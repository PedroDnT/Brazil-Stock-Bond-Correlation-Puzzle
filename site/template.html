A Floor,
Not a Spike

Brazilian government bonds are diversifiers, not hedges. Across two decades and six macro regimes the stock-bond correlation never turns reliably negative — and most of the crisis spikes everyone quotes dissolve once you correct for volatility.

The question

Is Brazil where advanced economies are heading?

The IMF's February 2026 study dates a structural breakdown in stock-bond diversification across the US, Germany, Japan and the UK to late 2019, and attributes it to post-pandemic supply shocks, fiscal expansion and quantitative tightening. Every one of those mechanisms has been a permanent feature of Brazilian finance for decades — plus a fourth the framework omits: sovereign credit risk, which can push equity valuations and government bond prices down together.

The natural conjecture is that Brazil already is the destination. This page tests that conjecture against 21 years of daily data rather than assuming it, and the answer is more qualified than the conjecture suggests. The distinguishing feature is not a high correlation or a crisis-time breakdown. It is the absence of any negative-correlation regime to revert to.

The sovereign channel implies a floor, not a level: it should stop the correlation turning reliably negative without necessarily driving it high. That is exactly what the data show.

Paper §4.3 · Table 5

The horizon matters more than any other modelling choice

Daily estimates are attenuated — the Ibovespa close, the Tesouro reference price and the CDI accrual are not struck at the same instant. Lengthen the return interval and the macro co-movement appears. For NTN-B the correlation more than triples from daily to monthly; for NTN-F it more than doubles to +0.473. The LFT, with near-zero duration, stays flat at every horizon, as it should.

Paper §6 · Table 8

Most crisis spikes are volatility artefacts

Forbes and Rigobon (2002) showed that measured correlation is conditional on market volatility: when the variance of the source market rises, the sample correlation is biased upward even if the propagation mechanism is unchanged. Brazilian crisis windows carry five to ten times calm-period equity variance, so the correction is not cosmetic.

Applied to six episodes, it reverses the standard narrative. The GFC shows no correlation elevation at all — raw correlation barely moves despite equity variance rising nearly ninefold, and the adjusted figure falls below the calm-period level. Roughly two thirds of COVID's apparent spike is heteroskedasticity. Only Joesley Day, a purely domestic political shock, shows unambiguous contagion.

Paper §5 · Tables 6 & 7

Where the diversification does fail: the daily left tail

Crisis windows are one horizon; single days are another. Conditional on the worst decile of equity days the NTN-B correlation more than doubles to +0.279, with an interval comfortably excluding zero. The copula evidence points the same way and adds asymmetry.

The Student-t copula wins on AIC for all four pairs, but it imposes equal upper and lower tail dependence by construction and therefore cannot represent what is actually there. The non-parametric exceedance rates can: Brazilian stocks and bonds crash together more often than they boom together.

And yet bonds cushioned four of six crises

Total return is misleading in a market with a 10–15% cash rate: a seven-month crisis window accrues roughly 7% of carry before any price moves, so a bond portfolio that “made money in the GFC” may simply have earned it. Measured in excess of CDI — the only benchmark that means anything domestically — NTN-B still delivered positive excess returns in four of the six episodes, including both large equity drawdowns.

The two episodes where bonds underperformed cash, Joesley and Fiscal24, are both domestic fiscal-political shocks. Shock type, not crisis per se, determines whether Brazilian bonds diversify.

Paper §8.4 · Table 13

Stressed VaR is a volatility story, not a correlation story

The 60/40's 99% ten-day VaR rises 2.8× from calm conditions to the COVID window. Almost none of that comes from correlation. Shrinking the full-sample correlation matrix all the way to equicorrelation — every ρ forced to 1 — raises the same VaR only from 7.5% to 8.3%, an increase of 11.3%. Because Brazilian domestic correlations start near zero rather than negative, the equicorrelation stress is a smaller shock here than it would be for a US 60/40 portfolio.

Paper §8.3

“The LFT” is not one asset

The Tesouro Selic is routinely described as a uniform domestic safe haven. Split it by maturity and that holds only at the short end. The distinction is invisible if you define the LFT return as compounded CDI, because such a series is monotonically increasing by construction — “the LFT never loses money” becomes a property of the definition rather than a finding. Built from observed unit prices the claim is falsifiable, and for long LFTs it fails.

The full-sample picture

All three duration-bearing bonds show small, precisely estimated positive correlations; the LFT is statistically indistinguishable from zero, as its near-zero duration implies. A correlation of +0.12 does not mean stocks and bonds move together in any dramatic sense — it means they are close to uncorrelated with a positive tilt, and an asset at ρ ≈ 0.12 still reduces portfolio variance substantially.

Every series rejects normality decisively — Jarque-Bera p < 10⁻¹⁵ throughout, with excess kurtosis from 4.6 to 28.4 and pronounced negative skew in every bond series. That is what motivates the copula and Expected Shortfall treatment rather than a Gaussian one.

Conclusions

Nine findings

  1. 1

    The correlation is positive, precisely estimated, and small. Ibovespa × NTN-B = +0.122 daily; LTN +0.134; NTN-F +0.147; LFT +0.005, indistinguishable from zero. Brazilian bonds are diversifiers, not hedges.

  2. 2

    Horizon dominates every other modelling choice. The correlation triples from +0.122 daily to +0.405 monthly. Cross-country comparisons must be made at matched frequency.

  3. 3

    There is a floor, not a regime shift. No regime shows a negative correlation and none is statistically distinguishable from the calmest one. The post-2013 upward drift is suggestive, not established.

  4. 4

    Most crisis spikes are volatility artefacts. After Forbes-Rigobon, the GFC, Americanas and Fiscal24 show no elevation; COVID's raw +0.370 falls to +0.124. Only Joesley Day is unambiguous contagion.

  5. 5

    Time variation in the DCC is marginal. Only the LTN pair rejects a = 0 at 5% (t = 2.24); NTN-B falls just short at t = 1.94. The DCC paths are reported as descriptive, not as evidence of an established time-varying process.

  6. 6

    Co-crash risk is real in the daily tail, and asymmetric. Empirical lower-tail dependence of 0.143 against 0.050 under independence, exceeding the upper-tail 0.094. Conditional on the worst decile of equity days, ρ doubles to +0.279.

  7. 7

    Bonds nevertheless cushioned four of six crises. NTN-B returned +5.8pp over CDI in the GFC and +1.3pp in COVID. It underperformed cash only in Joesley and Fiscal24 — both domestic fiscal-political shocks.

  8. 8

    The LFT safe-haven claim is maturity-dependent. 0.008% maximum drawdown for the 1-year over 21 years; 1.33% for the longest outstanding, across 73 negative days.

  9. 9

    Brazilian equities had a negative Sharpe ratio against CDI. 9.30% annualised, 26% volatility, 60% maximum drawdown, against a cash rate that exceeded it. This reframes what a domestic 60/40 portfolio is for.

What it implies for allocation

The operative question is not whether domestic bonds hedge equities. They do not, and no allocation within domestic fixed income will make them. It is whether the shock in prospect is global or domestic-fiscal. Duration diversifies global risk-off episodes and amplifies domestic fiscal-political ones, which makes the fiscal-regime indicator — primary balance trajectory, sovereign spread trend — the allocation switch. Against a domestic fiscal shock the hedges are short LFT and international assets; long LFT is not the risk-free instrument it is usually assumed to be.

Against the IMF framework: at matched monthly frequency Brazil's +0.41 sits squarely in the range reported for the post-2020 developed-market regime. Whether that constitutes convergence is a question only a matched cross-country panel could answer, and this study does not attempt one.

Reproduction

How every number here was produced

Data

All sources are free and public. The master dataset holds 5,614 daily rows from 2 January 2004 to 23 July 2026; bond series begin 3 January 2005.

Equities
Ibovespa daily close, IPEADATA GM366_IBVSP366.
Bonds
Constant-maturity total-return series from Tesouro Transparente daily unit prices — 5y NTN-B, 2y LTN, 10y NTN-F, 1y and longest-outstanding LFT.
Macro
CDI, Selic, IPCA, IGP-M and PTAX from the BCB SGS API.
Sovereign
EMBI+ Brazil in basis points, IPEADATA JPM366_EMBI366, to July 2024 when the series was discontinued.

Two construction details that change the answer

Rolls never inject a price jump. The daily return is that of the bond selected on the previous day, so a change in the selected maturity cannot register as a return. Coupons are added back on payment dates, with the face or inflation-updated VNA recovered from the bond's own quoted yield through a du/252 cashflow pricer — validated on NTN-F, whose face is known to be R$1,000: it recovers 1,001.6. Left untreated, a semiannual NTN-B coupon appears as a spurious one-day loss of about 2.9% of VNA, roughly 42 times over the sample.

The LFT is built from observed Tesouro Selic prices, not compounded CDI. As a validation check the 1-year LFT tracks independently sourced compounded CDI with a daily correlation of 0.958 and an annualised gap of +0.14pp.

Estimators

Pearson correlations with Fisher-z 95% intervals throughout; regime differences by stationary block bootstrap (21-day blocks, 1,500 replications). Forbes-Rigobon for crisis windows. Engle (2002) two-stage DCC(1,1) with Student-t innovations and standard errors from the numerical Hessian. Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton and Gumbel copulas fitted by maximum likelihood and ranked by AIC, with each density unit-tested to integrate to 1 over the unit square. Diversification Ratio, Effective Number of Bets and PC1 share on rolling 252-day windows; CoVaR by quantile regression.

Limitations

Bond series are constant-maturity constructions from Tesouro Direto retail reference prices, not institutional secondary-market quotes; realised tenor deviates from target by an average of 0.74 years for NTN-B and 1.11 years for NTN-F. Crisis windows are researcher-defined, and the Joesley window is only 11 trading days, so any statistic from it is imprecise. Regime boundaries come from political economy rather than being estimated — given the bootstrap results, a Markov-switching estimate might well fail to find distinct regimes at all. The global macro extension requires a FRED key and was not executed for this version, so the real-policy-rate and news-sentiment claims from earlier drafts are treated as open rather than established.
