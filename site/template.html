Why Brazil has a floor

Textbook stock-bond correlation is set by which shock dominates: growth shocks push stocks and bonds apart, inflation shocks push them together. Brazil adds a third channel the framework leaves out — sovereign credit risk. When fiscal credibility slips, equity valuations and government bond prices fall together, because both are pricing the same probability of adjustment, restructuring or monetisation.

That channel doesn't make the correlation large. It makes it never reliably negative. Brazilian bonds are diversifiers, not hedges — and the advanced economies the IMF wrote about are losing a hedge Brazil never had to lose.

What this changes about a Brazilian 60/40

Duration diversifies global risk-off and amplifies domestic fiscal stress, so no allocation inside domestic fixed income turns these bonds into a hedge. Against a home-grown fiscal shock the hedges are short LFT and international assets — and long LFT is not the risk-free instrument it is assumed to be, drawing down 1.33% at its longest maturity against 0.008% at one year.

The harder question the data raise is what the equity leg is for: the Ibovespa lost to cash over twenty-one years, at 26% volatility and a 60% drawdown. A domestic 60/40 was paying for equity risk out of a negative premium while holding a bond leg that co-crashes in the left tail.

Qualifications

  • No regime is statistically distinguishable from the calmest — all bootstrap p > 0.05, and regime boundaries are researcher-defined rather than estimated.
  • Convergence toward Brazil is directionally unanimous — four of four G4 gaps narrowed — but significant only for Germany. Four correlated bond markets moving together is closer to one observation than to four.
  • Joesley is an 11-day window, so that estimate is imprecise; the DCC time variation is weakly identified and reported as descriptive.
  • The earlier draft's real-rate and news-sentiment findings rested on a notebook that had never run and are not reported — those claims are open, not established.
